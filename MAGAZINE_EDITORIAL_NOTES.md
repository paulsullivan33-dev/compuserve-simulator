## Historical grounding

The magazine is newly authored fiction, not a recovered publication. Characters,
club incidents, prices used in arithmetic examples, and evaluation scenarios are
invented. Equipment discussions do not claim original bench testing. The voice
assumes a December 1988 reader; explicit notes about emulator imports describe
the present-day simulation's supplied BASIC files. No later software releases
are presented as available to the 1988 reader.

These references support specific historical or technical details, rather than
serving as sources for entire articles. No historical article or manual passage
was reproduced in the magazine.

- [Microsoft's MS-DOS Encyclopedia (1988), programming environment](https://www.pcjs.org/documents/books/mspl13/msdos/encyclopedia/section2/):
  period DOS context, conventional memory, and the distinction between hardware
  capacity and application access. Used for the DOS-memory discussion.
- [Apple's HyperCard User's Guide (1987)](https://vintageapple.org/macmanuals/pdf/HyperCard_Users_Guide_1987.pdf):
  cards, fields, buttons, and shared backgrounds, including the background
  example on printed page 42. Used for the fictional book-index project.
- [Commodore's VIC-1541 user manual](https://www.commodore.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/commodore_vic_1541_floppy_drive_users_manual.pdf):
  reference for the C64/1541 loading and directory context. The article directs
  readers to the manual rather than giving universal disk commands.
- [QuestBusters, volume V, issue 7 (1988)](https://mocagh.org/questbusters/qbustersV7.pdf):
  contemporaneous evidence of Wasteland discussion and differing machine
  versions. The magazine does not reproduce its review or give game solutions.
- [Atari ST owner's manual scan](https://www.scribd.com/document/710159634/ATARI-ST-Computer):
  the manual's MIDI IN/OUT descriptions corroborate the ST music-workshop context.
- [Computer History Museum: Desktop Publishing](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/personal-computers/17/306):
  historical context for PageMaker and Macintosh desktop publishing.
- [Computer History Museum: Electronic Arts DeluxePaint early source code](https://computerhistory.org/blog/electronic-arts-deluxepaint-early-source-code/):
  primary software-preservation material establishing DeluxePaint's availability
  before the issues' dates.
- [The Computer Paper, June 1988](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2023/11/102803712-05-006-acc.pdf):
  contemporaneous coverage supports treating computer viruses as a concern
  already discussed in 1988. The fictional disk-exchange article gives no
  unsupported statistics or invented infection diagnosis.

Program-specific statements about DOCK64, ORCHARD, LOSTDISK, TEMPO, and CALLCOST
refer to this project's `computer_communities.json` and its supplied instructions,
not commercial programs or historical software archives.
