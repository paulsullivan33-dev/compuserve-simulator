# Reconstructed December 1988 computer catalog

The thirteen configurations and 27 computer accessories are simulated
retail bundles, not transcriptions of Comp-U-Store offers. All prices, availability,
shipping, and included bundle combinations are fictional. Generic XT/AT-compatible
packages represent period configurations, rather than a specific manufacturer's model.

Primary reference material consulted:

- [Apple Macintosh Plus specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/112183):
  1986 introduction, 8 MHz 68000, 1MB minimum RAM, 800K floppy, external SCSI,
  built-in display, no ADB. The catalog's Plus input devices are kept separate from SE ADB devices.
- [Apple Macintosh SE specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/112185):
  1987 introduction, 8 MHz 68000, 1MB minimum RAM, ADB, 800K floppy and internal SCSI storage.
  The simulated SE bundle uses a 20MB disk; it is not a later SE/30 or FDHD.
- [Intel's 80286 history](https://timeline.intel.com/1982/the-286-changes-personal-computing):
  1982 processor introduction and use in the 1984 IBM PC AT and compatible computers.
- [Commodore manufacturer family brochure, archived by Commodore.ca](https://www.commodore.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Meet-The-Commodore-Family-C64-C128-PC10-PC20-PC40-Amiga-500-PC5-Amiga-1000-Amiga-2000.pdf):
  64C and Amiga family configurations. Search index readable; direct PDF retrieval unavailable.
- [Commodore A500 service manual, June 1987, archived scan](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/4225732/Commodore-Computers-A500.html):
  68000 processor, 512K RAM expandable to 1MB, 880K disks and RS-232 interface.

Accessory descriptions state important interface requirements and parts already
included in bundles. Compatibility indicates catalog suitability, sometimes
conditional on the prerequisites in the description. GO SETUP models selected
resource limits, not instruction-level hardware emulation.

Additional manufacturer manuals used for the expansion:

- [Apple IIe Technical Reference, archived scan](https://www.applelogic.org/files/AIIETECHREF3.pdf):
  extended 80-column card and 128K configuration.
- [Apple IIgs Technical Procedures, archived scan](https://mirrors.apple2.org.za/Apple%20II%20Documentation%20Project/Computers/Apple%20II/Apple%20IIGS/Manuals/Apple%20IIGS%20Technical%20Procedures.pdf):
  2.8 MHz processor and 256K base memory. Our ROM 01 bundle adds a 1MB expansion;
  it is not the later ROM 3 machine.
- [Atari 1040ST Owner's Manual, archived scan](https://manuals.plus/m/5a3cf0624c2c7797670c655a0f6d53399a78853f77580a4b71f451c155c8afa7):
  ST peripherals and one supported external floppy drive.
- [Tandy 1000 HX manufacturer brochure, archived scan](https://manuals.plus/m/9af2d7c144c240dc4316291bb781bc20d0f5efbf74f89fd5a0c97dc00b5c649a.pdf):
  256K base, built-in DOS, 720K drive and PLUS expansion. Our bundle includes
  an expanded 640K configuration.
- [Tandy 102 Owner's Manual, archived scan](https://ftp.whtech.com/club100/doc/tandy102.pdf):
  portable operation and built-in 300-baud modem.

Download budgets, transfer overhead factors, prices and retailer bundles are
simulation choices. They are not claims about exact free space or historical offers.
